# Conclusion — concepts

## Claims

- The promise in `topic.md` is five capabilities; this section keeps exactly
  those five and adds none.
- The three open questions are genuine, not rhetorical. Each is something the
  author cannot currently settle.

## Decisions

- Capabilities are written as actions the reader takes, not topics they have
  seen. This is the standard `topic.md` sets and the one the panel will check.
- The open questions are stated rather than hidden. They are also the likeliest
  Q&A topics, so naming them means the author meets those questions having
  already thought about them — and a known unknown does not read as a gap.
- "An observation you actually make may beat an automated test you never run" is
  a concession against the tutorial's own preference for executable checks. It
  stays. Conceding the strongest counter-argument is cheaper here than being
  caught by it.
- No summary slide restating the three families. At this length the audience saw
  them four minutes ago.

## Open questions

- Does the deck need a closing line that is not a list? A talk that ends on a
  bulleted capability list ends flat, and the last sentence spoken is the one the
  room carries out.

## Not doing

- An AI-use disclosure slide. The disclosure belongs with the submitted artifact
  rather than in the ten-minute deck, and its placement is still unconfirmed.
- Further reading. Nothing in the reference list would survive the time budget,
  and the pre-shared markdown can carry links the talk cannot.
